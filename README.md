# Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence

This repository collects selected academic essays written during my Bachelor’s degree in **Philosophy and Artificial Intelligence** at Sapienza University of Rome.

My work explores the philosophical foundations of artificial intelligence, with particular attention to the relationship between **mind, cognition, technology, and AI governance**.
These essays reflect an interdisciplinary perspective that connects **philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and artificial intelligence**, an approach that continues to inform my current Master's studies in **Human-Centered AI / NeuroAI**.

## Research Themes

The essays included in this repository address several core philosophical questions related to artificial intelligence:

* The relationship between **human cognition and artificial systems**
* The philosophical implications of **Explainable AI and transparency**
* Trust, opacity, and interpretability in AI systems
* The interaction between **AI regulation, ethics, and epistemology**
* Theoretical perspectives from **philosophy of mind and cognitive science**

## Essays Included

**Cognition, 4E Theories, and the Dogma of Harmony**
A philosophical analysis of 4E cognition (embodied, embedded, extended, and enactive cognition) and the assumptions of harmony between cognitive agents and their environments. The essay critically examines whether cognitive systems — biological or artificial — can be understood as naturally aligned with the structures of their environments.

**AI Governance, GDPR, and the Right to Opacity**
A critical examination of the relationship between the EU AI Act and the GDPR. The paper explores tensions between transparency requirements and epistemic autonomy, proposing the concept of a *right to opacity* as a philosophical counterbalance to dominant transparency narratives in AI governance.

**Hilary Putnam and Functionalism**
An analysis of Hilary Putnam’s functionalist theory of mind and its implications for artificial intelligence, focusing on the computational interpretation of mental states and the relationship between functionalism and machine intelligence.

**The New Paradigm of Transparency in Artificial Intelligence**
A philosophical critique of the growing emphasis on transparency and explainability in AI systems, questioning whether transparency alone can provide a sufficient foundation for trustworthy and accountable AI.
